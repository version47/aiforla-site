A Statewide AI Community for Louisiana

Pilot Project Brief

We have selected the Baton Rouge market for a pilot of our learning model: one cohort of 20 to 30 businesses, four sessions, October through December, by application.

Prepared By: Wes Kennison, Alex Hardee
01

Market Foundation

Initiative Context

Baton Rouge is about as AI-curious as any city in the country right now. When Anthropic ran its road show, the strongest registration response on the whole tour reportedly came from right here. And Baton Rouge certainly isn’t alone. For the first time, perhaps ever, small, scrappy business owners the world over are dealing with the same nagging question;

“I know AI represents opportunity, but what does that opportunity mean to MY business, and where do I start?”

As the world moves from being a business community in it’s infancy with AI to being AI integrated, shepherding that transition by teaching, sharing, and guiding is what this initiative represents.

Audience / Market Snapshot

Small and mid-sized business owners and operators are the micro-engines of the economy, changes to their quality of life directly impact local economies at the grassroots level.

The AI-narrative for SMB is nothing like what’s playing out in big enterprise.

The headlines for AI integration at the enterprise level are all about cutting headcount to hack growth on the balance sheet, but a small business team typically can’t afford to lose ANY of it’s team members who are actively engaged in the work they do, day in and day out.

We believe that the AI Opportunity for Small to Medium size businesses is two fold:

  1. 1Efficiency creation by streamlining and automating operations
  2. 2Leveraging this newly liberated internal expertise to apply it to new market challenges, so as to better compete with bigger, less nimble teams.

Brand Positioning

A rising tide raises all ships.

This is a community that runs on the open-source spirit. When Louisiana businesses win, we all win. The offer to business owners is simple,

“integrating AI into your business operations frees up more time, more capacity, and more room for the human parts of the work.

Our events and the learning track presented on the website are a jumping off point for you to begin your journey into building an Operating System for your business that optimizes how the work gets done.”

Organizing Principles

Context is the whole game.

AI with bad context is worse than a bad employee. With rich, specific context, it’s a different tool entirely. So adoption has to happen inside each person’s real work, this is the essence of practitioner led change.

The hump is smaller than it looks, and most of it is in your head.

The first and most difficult barrier to entry is taking the first step. Getting a business from 0 to 1 means facilitating a transition from chatting with AI in a browser to throwing your day-to-day work at AI in an integrated and secure way.

There’s no silver bullet.

You don’t “install AI” and wake up optimized. Working with AI is a series of cycles, full of dips and nooks and operational uncertainty. Every time you run a piece of real work through AI inside good guardrails, that curve gets a little smoother. Do it enough and the day gets more dependable, because everything you touch is captured in one place you can talk to. When you commit to the work it takes to get AI integrated, each success builds on the one before it and compounds the longer you work it.

Strategy

The core idea for building sustainable economic lift comes in the form of “Practitioner-Led Change”.

The working practitioner, in any industry vertical, is the best source of the accurate context that their vertical needs to be effective in adopting AI based change.

This expertise allows for anyone working in that industry to see themselves in the solutions being put together, and incentivizes others to adapt similar measures.

As more and more successes happen across verticals, the network effect of a more optimized business landscape can begin to emerge, making for a more vibrant and thriving business community.

Tone / Voice

We are authentic, unvarnished, peer-to-peer.

No hype.

No shortcut-to-revenue energy.

No cutting-headcount language.

This community represents an avenue and a proving ground for creating new opportunity and working through what implementation really looks like in real world terms.

Getting Started

Most small business owners are scrappy and entrepreneurial, they don’t need to be motivated, they just need to be pointed in the right direction and things can begin to shift. This makes real the claim that the free resources provided can be enough to put an operator on a track to revolutionize their business, in short order.

Stage 1 · Learn (free, online)

An online learn page that features 3 levels of learning content

Level 1

What is Claude?

Level 2

I’m tired of copying and pasting results out of an LLM and I’m ready to learn about AI Integration.

Level 3

I’m interested in building skills, MCPs, and generally optimizing what I’ve got working.

The content will be largely curated and featured in 3 playlists, which are updated every week as new things emerge in the technology landscape.

Stage 2 · Events (free, in person)

Recurring, topical, attendee-led gatherings, the vision is for anyone who owns a business can come. You just get more out of it once you’ve done the levels, because your business has its own things to learn first. The on-ramp is a sign at an intersection: “Business owner?” and a QR code straight to the Learn page. Formats survey the room, pull in challenges people sent ahead of time so the content stays current, and give everyone a place to ask questions and find resources.

Stage 3 · Community

Conversations are where we learn from each other, owners who’ve gotten to “I can do this” start talking to each other, comparing what they tried, what broke, what worked. That discourse is the whole point. It sustains itself and it grows: people who’ve made the jump bring in the next curious person, and the room gets bigger on its own.

Event Formats

Two to three formats on a rotation, plus webinars:

  • Learn — AI theory and applied; get people to the goal line.
  • Mingle — a happy hour with a short peer-testimonial slot: “here’s what I did in my business.”
  • Roundtable — a light panel of operators comparing what they’re trying.

The Baton Rouge Pilot Cohort

The pilot runs as one cohort, not a series of open events. Twenty to thirty businesses are accepted by application, and one application covers all four sessions.

The four sessions alternate Learn at midday and Mingle in the evening, and they work as one arc: learn something, try it in your business, come back and talk about it. Owners bring a laptop and a Claude or ChatGPT account.
